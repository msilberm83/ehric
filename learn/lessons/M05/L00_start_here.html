Module 5 — Start here: outline and the problem

Chapter outline

  1. Tie training to the approved build. Start from the Module 4 traceability record. Every training objective traces to an approved workflow requirement.
  2. Plan by role. Group learners by the work they do and the access they will have. You produce a role-based training plan.
  3. Train super users first. Ask the clinic to name super users for each role and location, with protected time. Train and check them before other staff. You produce a super-user roster.
  4. Plan for every learner. Ask the client's training lead early about accommodations, language needs, and shifts. The client decides accommodations. You produce accessible job aids, plain-language steps, and captioned recordings.
  5. Prepare the training environment. Confirm the environment, release ID, technical acceptance, logins, synthetic data, and job aids. You produce an environment and release record.
  6. Time final training close to go-live. Use early sessions to validate workflows with key users. Hold final end-user training as close to go-live as the calendar allows, on the tested release that will go live. You produce a training schedule tied to the go-live date.
  7. Compensate when there is no separate training environment. Lay out the approved alternatives and add controls. You produce a written, approved environment decision.
  8. Write observable objectives. State what the learner will do, not what they will "understand." You produce session plans.
  9. Run the training loop. Show one complete scenario. Ask how it compares with real work. Set up only approved low-risk items in class. Have learners practice, then teach back and recover from an exception.
  10. Teach the general workflow families. Cover first contact, scheduling, intake, forms and signatures, and queues, with normal and exception cases.
  11. Record competence. Watch each learner complete a normal scenario and an exception alone. Attendance alone is not competence. You produce attendance and competence records, with rechecks scheduled.
  12. Route open items and request QC. Sort every question into training question, coaching need, defect, new requirement, policy decision, or homework. Give each an owner and a date. Send changed configuration to quality control (QC). You produce an open-item register and QC requests.

You will produce:

  • a role-based training plan, a super-user roster, and session plans built from approved scenarios;
  • an environment and release record, including any approved alternative to a separate training environment;
  • a training schedule tied to the go-live date and the release that will go live;
  • attendance and demonstrated-competence records;
  • an open-item register and QC requests.

Checked off when: every required learner has shown competence on normal and exception scenarios in the identified, accepted release that will go live, and every open item has an owner and a date.

The problem

Cedar Point Internal Medicine goes live in ten weeks. The practice manager wants all training "done and out of the way" this week. The build team is still fixing the intake forms. Her plan is one long class for twelve front-desk, clinical, and billing staff. The vendor has given the clinic only one production copy of the system. No one at the clinic has been named to help peers on the first day. Two staff members speak English as a second language, and one uses a screen reader.

Before you read: If the front desk trains this week, what will they still know on go-live day? Who should the clinic train first, and why?
