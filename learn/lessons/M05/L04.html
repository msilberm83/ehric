Worked example: front-desk training at Cedar Point

Cedar Point goes live in five weeks. You are the vendor's implementation specialist. You must deliver final front-desk training at the main clinic and Lakeshore.

  1. Traced objectives to requirements. You pulled every front-desk requirement from the Module 4 traceability record. Produced: six observable objectives, each linked to a requirement. Why: untraced training wastes time, and untrained requirements fail at go-live.
  2. Confirmed super users. You asked the practice manager for super users. She named the front-desk lead at main and a senior scheduler at Lakeshore, each with four protected hours a week. Produced: a super-user roster. Why: they will support peers on go-live day, so the clinic must choose them and free their time.
  3. Recorded the release. You confirmed that release 2.3, the go-live release, passed QC and technical acceptance. Produced: an environment and release record. Why: competence evidence counts only on an identified, accepted release.
  4. Trained super users first. One week before the end-user sessions, you trained both super users. Both passed the normal and exception scenarios and practiced sorting questions. Produced: two competence records. Why: they can now co-lead practice and answer peers' questions.
  5. Scheduled final training. You booked end-user sessions three and two weeks before go-live, with a repeat session for the early shift. Produced: a training schedule tied to the go-live date. Why: staff remember more, and they learn the screens that will go live.
  6. Ran the loop. Staff practiced a new appointment, a changed address, a likely duplicate, an unsigned intake packet, and a same-day cancellation. All records were synthetic. Produced: guided and independent practice results. Why: normal and exception cases show whether staff can recover.
  7. Logged a defect. A correctly completed form entered the billing queue. You logged a defect and used the approved temporary route for the exercise. You did not change the build in class. Produced: a defect entry and a QC request. Why: a build fault needs a tested fix, not more practice.
  8. Recorded competence. Ten of twelve learners passed both scenarios. You scheduled private rechecks for two. After QC fixed the form routing, affected learners repeated that scenario. Produced: competence records and rechecks. Why: the clinic needs proof for each learner on the release that will go live.

Prompt 1: Why did you train the super users a week before the other front-desk staff?

Prompt 2: Why did you log the form problem as a defect instead of asking the learner to try again?

Prompt 3: Why did affected learners repeat the scenario after QC fixed the routing?

Your turn: faded example

If you have done this task on a real project, try this first, then compare with the worked example.

A three-provider pediatric clinic goes live in four weeks. The vendor offers only one production copy. Conversion loaded real patient data last week. You must train four medical assistants on check-in and the intake packet.

  1. Traced objectives to requirements. You found three approved check-in and intake requirements. Produced: three observable objectives. Why: every objective must point to an approved requirement.
  2. Confirmed super users. The clinic named its lead medical assistant as super user, with two protected hours a week. Produced: a super-user roster. Why: the clinic chooses super users and protects their time.
  3. Checked the environment options. Pre-live production is gone, because real data is already loaded. Produced: a list of the remaining options and their risks. Why: the client cannot choose well without the options in front of it.
  4. Your step: What controls do you list, and who decides which option to use? What do you produce?
  5. Your step: In what order and when do you train the super user and the other medical assistants? What do you produce, and why?
  6. Your step: In class, one learner checks in a test patient, and a real reminder text goes out. How do you classify and route it?
  7. Your step: What do you record for each learner before the gate can close?

Field assignment

Plan and deliver a general-workflow training session for the practice case, Cedar Point Internal Medicine (main clinic and Lakeshore). Use synthetic data only. Do not use a real client, employer, or patient.

  1. Define role groups and prerequisites.
  2. Write at least four observable learning objectives, each traced to a requirement.
  3. Draft the super-user roster request: roles, locations, shifts, and protected hours.
  4. Prepare the environment, synthetic data, and job aids. If no separate training environment exists, write the environment decision and its controls.
  5. Record the release ID and technical-acceptance evidence.
  6. Write a training schedule that places final end-user training close to go-live, with refreshers for changed workflows.
  7. Check one job aid for accessibility and plain language, and plan captions for any recording.
  8. Demonstrate one normal scenario, then lead guided and independent practice with at least two exceptions.
  9. Record attendance and demonstrated competence.
  10. Classify and route every open item, and request QC for changed configuration.

Self-check rubric. Your work is ready when:

Knowledge check

  1. Why should training be organized by role rather than by product menu?
  2. What is the difference between attendance and competence?
  3. How does the course keep "set up as you go" without allowing uncontrolled changes?
  4. Why should training use approved scenarios from discovery?
  5. Who approves controlled form content and professional workflow decisions?
  6. What evidence shows that a learner can recover from an exception?
  7. Why should formal training identify the environment and release being taught?
  8. A small vendor has only one production copy. Who decides how training will run, and what controls do you document?
  9. Who decides what accommodation a learner receives, and what do you record?
  10. Why does final end-user training happen close to go-live rather than right after the build?
  11. Who chooses super users, and why are they trained before other staff?

Spaced review

  1. (Module 4) During Cedar Point training, staff say, "We must scan every ID card at every visit; it's the law." What do you record before anyone builds or trains that step?
  2. (Module 4) A learner asks for a new feature the clinic has wanted for years. It is helpful but not needed to go live. Which class of change is it, and where does it go?
  3. (Module 2) The practice manager wants to move the go-live date one week earlier. The date's basis label is "target." How does that label change your next step?
  4. (Module 1) The signed order lists eight training seats, but the sales notes promise "unlimited training." How do you sort this commitment, and what do you log?
  5. (Module 3) The kickoff record has not gone out three days after the meeting. Why is that a problem, and what was the rule?
