Module 1 — Start here: outline and the problem

Chapter outline

  1. Learn what you own. You own the connection between the sale, the scope, the team, and the plan. Other people own clinical, billing, privacy, security, and business decisions. You produce a list of who is responsible, accountable, consulted, and informed for each major item.
  2. Collect the commercial record before the handoff. Ask sales for the signed order, statement of work, proposal, approved changes, and sales notes. Ask for the results of the clinic's buying work, such as its goals and any readiness assessment. You produce a document list, with missing items logged.
  3. Run the handoff meeting. Cover six topics in order: why the client bought, what was bought, who decides, what was promised, what could go wrong, and the next action. You produce a handoff record.
  4. Sort every commitment. Mark each promise as documented (in the signed order, statement of work, an incorporated proposal, or an approved change) or remembered (sales notes, emails, verbal). You produce a commitments table with an owner and due date for each remembered item.
  5. Write the initial scope summary. Split the work into in scope, out of scope, and to be confirmed. You produce the scope summary.
  6. State how you will know it works. Write early acceptance evidence for each major item. For each purchased outcome, agree how success will be measured and who owns the measure. You produce acceptance statements and a success-measure list.
  7. Build the client and project profile. Gather only facts that help the team plan. Place privacy and security questions with the client's authority. You produce a one-page profile.
  8. Derive the resource map from the sale. Turn each purchased item into the expertise it needs and the first question to ask. You produce a resource map with a named accountable person for each item.
  9. Open the control logs. Start the risk, assumption, issue, and dependency log, and a decision log. You produce at least one entry of each type, each with an owner.
  10. Check the gate. Confirm the handoff is complete enough for planning to start in Module 2.

You will produce

  • A sales-to-implementation handoff record, including a commitments table
  • An initial scope summary with acceptance evidence and success measures
  • A client and project profile, and a first resource map
  • The first risk, assumption, issue, dependency, and decision log entries

Checked off when every item on the Module 1 gate checklist is true and the project can move into detailed planning.

The problem

Cedar Point Internal Medicine has two locations. It has just signed for a new electronic health record (EHR), the system that holds patient charts. The salesperson books a 30-minute call to "hand you the client." On the call, she says the practice expects a lab interface, two extra training days, and a January go-live. You have not seen the signed order yet. The next morning, the practice administrator emails you. She asks when the lab interface will be ready.

Before you read: Which of those three expectations can you treat as scope today, and how would you know?
