Module 13 — Start here: outline and the problem

Chapter outline

Module 12 ended with stabilization exit, a transfer to operations, and a closed implementation scope. This chapter covers the post-live period that many contracts include after that point. It ends when you leave the client with owned measures, a working improvement process, and a formally closed engagement. The steps below are in the order the chapter teaches them.

  1. Confirm the steady-state handoff. Check that support tickets now go to the clinic's operating owners. Produce a dated handoff note.
  2. Set the support model and service levels. Write down severity, response, update, and escalation rules. Keep vendor contract terms apart from internal targets. Produce a service-level table.
  3. Plan the 30, 60, and 90-day reviews. Give each review a purpose, a data set, and a decision.
  4. Choose adoption measures the clinic can own. Pick a small set, define each one, and name its owner. Produce a measure sheet.
  5. Compare results with the pre-live baseline. Use the same definitions and population. Report changes in method beside the result. Produce a benefits comparison.
  6. Run the optimization backlog. Take requests through one intake route, sort them, and prioritize with the client's governance group. Send approved changes through change control.
  7. Keep training and the super-user network going. Plan short, role-based refresher sessions and new-hire onboarding. Keep the super-user network active.
  8. Manage vendor releases and upgrades. Read release notes for impact, run the regression set, tell users, and watch the system after the change.
  9. Run or repeat the SAFER self-assessment. Help the clinic's own team rate the High Priority Practices guide after go-live.
  10. Record lessons and close the engagement. Hold a lessons session, name every open item and its owner, and get client sign-off.
  11. Handle renewal and expansion talk honestly. Report facts, send buying questions to the account owner, and never tie fixes to a sale.

You will produce:

  • a steady-state handoff note and a service-level table (practice case);
  • a post-live measure sheet with baseline, owner, and review dates (practice case);
  • 30, 60, and 90-day review records, with a benefits comparison (practice case);
  • an optimization backlog with priorities and change-control status (practice case);
  • a refresher training plan, a super-user role description, and a release record (practice case);
  • a post-live SAFER self-assessment plan and an engagement closure record (practice case).

All of this work uses the course practice case, Cedar Point Internal Medicine (main clinic and Lakeshore), or the casebook. Do not use a real client, employer, or patient data.

Checked off when: each measure has an owner and a baseline, the 90-day review is recorded, the backlog is owned by the clinic, and the client has signed the closure record.

The problem

Cedar Point has been live for 60 days. Unsigned notes have dropped sharply. But at Lakeshore, only 9 percent of patients have activated the patient portal. At the main clinic, the figure is 22 percent.

The sponsor asks you a direct question: "Should we buy the vendor's patient-engagement add-on to fix Lakeshore?" The practice manager has noticed that Lakeshore's front desk does not offer the portal at check-in.
