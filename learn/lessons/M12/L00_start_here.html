Module 12 — Start here: outline and the problem

Chapter outline

This chapter takes you from the approved go-live decision to a closed implementation. Go-live is the first day the clinic uses the new system for real patient work. Your job is to keep issues visible, keep decisions with the right people, and keep work reconciled until the clinic can run the system on its own. The steps below are in the order the chapter teaches them.

  1. Confirm the decision still holds. Run the final pre-start check. Record who authorized activation, and when.
  2. Open the command function. Set up one shared issue record and the channels that feed it.
  3. Triage by impact. Sort each report by risk, reach, and whether work is blocked. Name who decides.
  4. Control changes during go-live. Send urgent fixes through the emergency change path. Produce a change record for each one.
  5. Reconcile each operating period. Compare expected and completed work every day. Produce a reconciliation result.
  6. Run the end-of-day huddle. Review the day, the overnight risk, and whether the decision still holds. Issue a short status.
  7. Use downtime or pause when required. Follow the approved procedure, record paper work, and get authority to resume.
  8. End stabilization on evidence. Apply exit criteria by workflow and risk, not by the calendar. Produce an exit review.
  9. Follow up on a schedule. Review the first day, first week, and first month against the purchased outcomes.
  10. Transfer to operations and support. Hand over ownership and a support knowledge pack. Have the receivers demonstrate the tasks.
  11. Close the implementation. Confirm scope status, remove temporary access, name owners for open work, and record lessons.

After the gate, a separate Course requirements section explains the capstone and the certificate. Those are course steps, not implementation steps.

You will produce:

  • a go-live command and issue record, with daily reconciliation and huddle status (practice case);
  • a stabilization exit review (practice case);
  • an operations transfer record with a support knowledge pack (practice case);
  • a closure record that names every open item and its owner (practice case);
  • your three capstone artifacts, submitted for review.

All practice work uses the course practice case, Cedar Point Internal Medicine, or the casebook. Do not use a real client, employer, or patient data.

Checked off when: the practice-case launch is reconciled, stabilized, transferred, and closed on evidence, and your capstone artifacts are submitted.

The problem

Cedar Point Internal Medicine goes live on Monday at 07:00. On Friday, the practice administrator approved go-live. At 06:10 on Monday, a test message to the Lakeshore lab results interface fails. The vendor says it can probably fix it within an hour.

Ten days later, support tickets are low. The sponsor wants to end the period of extra support. But no one has checked the reminder error list since day 4. Lakeshore still keeps a paper referral log.

Before you read, try to answer:

Does Friday's approval still hold at 06:30 on Monday? Who decides?

Do low ticket counts on day 10 show that the extra support can end?
