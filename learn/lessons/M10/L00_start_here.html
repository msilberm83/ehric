Module 10 — Start here: outline and the problem

Chapter outline

  1. Write a use-case statement for every proposed automation or artificial intelligence (AI) use. You produce one statement per use that names the owner, data, action, result, and control.
  2. Check that the workflow is ready. Test each use against the approved future-state map from Module 4. You produce a short readiness check.
  3. Sort each use as rules-based or AI-enabled. The two need different tests. You produce the classification.
  4. Build the use-case inventory. You produce one inventory row per use, from owner and purpose to retirement plan.
  5. Run the risk and authority review. Ask about effect, information, authority, performance, and operations. You produce a recorded decision by an accountable owner for each use.
  6. Review the vendor and the deployment with the client's privacy, security, and contract owners. You produce a vendor and data decision record.
  7. Collect decision-support source information. For decision support that the vendor supplies in certified software, you gather the facts the federal rule requires and record the gaps. You pass them to the client's decision-support owner. You do not judge them.
  8. Design human oversight. Decide who reviews, when, and with what time and authority. You produce a review pattern for each use.
  9. Build a representative evaluation. Set thresholds before the final run. You produce a test set, thresholds, results by category, and a traceability record.
  10. Test rules-based automation for triggers, duplicates, timing, routing, chains, and stop. You produce test results and defects.
  11. Apply the domain controls for ambient notes, intake, scheduling, and billing help. You produce use-specific test cases and limits.
  12. Set up monitoring after release. You produce measures, owners, a review schedule, and pause or rollback thresholds.
  13. Check the plan against the 2025 safety self-assessment guides. You produce a completed crosswalk for the client's AI governance owner.

You will produce:

  • an automation and AI use-case inventory with risk and authority decisions;
  • a vendor, data, and decision-support source record;
  • an evaluation plan, results by category, and a release decision;
  • a monitoring, incident, stop, and training record;
  • trained owners and reviewers for each released use.

Checked off when: every released use has an approved owner, has met thresholds set in advance, and has named people who can stop it at any hour.

The problem

Two weeks before testing starts, the Cedar Point Internal Medicine practice manager sends you a short list. "Turn on the AI note tool for all providers. Turn on the AI sorter for scanned documents. And turn on the automatic refill reminders. The vendor says all three are certified and ready."

The clinical lead is excited about the note tool. Nobody has said who will check the AI drafts. Nobody knows what patient data the vendor keeps. Nobody knows who can turn a tool off at 2 a.m. on a Sunday.

Pre-questions: Which of the three uses would you test the same way, and which would you test differently? Who should decide whether the AI note tool is safe to use at Cedar Point?
