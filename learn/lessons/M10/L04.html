Worked example: ambient note pilot

A three-provider family medicine clinic wants to turn on an ambient note tool for every provider at once. Here is how the implementation specialist handled it.

  1. Did: wrote the use-case statement and proposed a pilot with three providers for six weeks instead of a full launch. Produced: a signed use-case statement and pilot scope. Why: a small pilot limits harm while the team gathers evidence.
  2. Did: ran the risk and authority review with the clinical, privacy, security, legal, records, billing, and technical owners. Produced: a decision record naming who approved each area. Why: each owner decides their own area, and the specialist only coordinates.
  3. Did: sent the vendor's data terms and business associate agreement to the privacy and contract owners. Produced: a vendor and data decision record. Why: visit audio is PHI, so those owners must approve how the vendor keeps and uses it.
  4. Did: built an evaluation set covering different visit types, accents, interpreter-assisted visits, interruptions, negation, medications, measurements, follow-up instructions, and a service outage. The clinical lead set thresholds before the run. Produced: test set version 1 with thresholds by category. Why: thresholds set after the results can be bent to fit them.

Prompt 1: Why do you think the specialist built the evaluation set before choosing the pilot providers' start date?

  1. Did: set the review pattern: providers correct and sign every draft before it is used, and drafts cannot trigger billing. Produced: a review pattern in the inventory. Why: the clinician stays responsible for the signed note.
  2. Did: ran the pilot and reviewed corrections by category each week. Produced: a correction log by visit type. Why: an overall rate could hide one bad visit type.
  3. Did: found that in interpreter-assisted visits the tool often credited the interpreter's words to the patient. The specialist took the finding to the clinical lead. The clinical lead excluded interpreter-assisted visits until a vendor fix passes retesting. Produced: a recorded limitation and the lead's decision. Why: limiting clinical use is a clinical decision.

Prompt 2: Why did the clinical lead, not the specialist, decide to exclude interpreter-assisted visits?

  1. Did: updated training and added a monitoring check for wrong-speaker corrections. Produced: revised training material and a new monitoring measure with an owner. Why: staff need to know the limit, and the team needs to see if the problem returns.

Prompt 3: Why does the new monitoring measure track wrong-speaker corrections by visit type instead of one overall correction rate?

A two-location pediatric clinic wants an AI service to sort incoming scanned documents into categories: lab report, referral letter, school form, and other. The first three steps are done. Complete steps 4 to 8.

  1. Did: wrote the use-case statement. "When a scanned document enters the intake queue, the AI service proposes a category. Front-desk staff confirm or correct it before routing. The office manager reviews corrections monthly." Produced: a signed statement. Why: it names the owner, data, action, result, and control.

Field assignment

Work from the Cedar Point Internal Medicine practice case. Do not use a real client, employer, or patient, even with names removed. Choose one rules-based automation and one AI use:

  1. Write the use-case statements.
  2. Confirm the approved future workflow and the success measure.
  3. Complete the use-case inventory and the risk and authority review.
  4. Record the vendor, data, contract, security, and privacy decisions. If the AI use is vendor-supplied decision support, record its source attributes.
  5. Define human review, forbidden actions, fallback, and stop method.
  6. Build a representative test set and set thresholds in advance.
  7. Test normal, incomplete, exception, misleading, access, outage, duplicate, and recovery conditions.
  8. Record versions, results, corrections, and the release decision.
  9. Train users and reviewers.
  10. Start the monitoring and incident record, and complete the SAFER AI crosswalk.

Self-check rubric. Your work is ready when:

Knowledge check

  1. What information must a usable automation or AI use-case statement contain?
  2. Why should deterministic automation and AI be evaluated differently?
  3. What makes human oversight effective?
  4. Why must thresholds be defined before the final evaluation?
  5. What information should be recorded so behavior can be compared after a model or feature change?
  6. What conditions should trigger pause, rollback, or retirement?
  7. What source information must a certified EHR make available for a vendor-supplied predictive decision support intervention, and who decides whether to activate it?
  8. Which SAFER AI item is most often missed by a stop method that depends on the vendor?

Spaced review
