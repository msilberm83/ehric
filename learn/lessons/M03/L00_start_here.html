Module 3 — Start here: outline and the problem

Chapter outline

This chapter teaches you to prepare, run, and record the client kickoff: the first meeting where the client and the implementation team agree how the project will work. You leave it with a working schedule. The kickoff follows eight sections in a fixed order. You check off each section with linked evidence. The steps below are in the order the chapter teaches them.

  1. Hold an internal readiness review. Confirm scope, dates, owners, and open questions with your own team before the client joins. You produce a readiness checklist and a go or reschedule decision.
  2. Choose participants by purpose. Invite the people whose authority the kickoff needs, not everyone who will ever help. You produce the participant list and ask about accommodations.
  3. Open in a fixed order. State the purpose, and handle early-leaver decisions first. You produce a filled-in agenda.
  4. Confirm outcome, scope, and the reason for change. Ask the client to correct your scope summary. Ask the sponsor to state why the clinic is changing. Send new requests to the change process. You produce confirmed scope and a list of open items.
  5. Confirm team and authority. Name each owner and the escalation bypass. You produce a named role list.
  6. Walk the roadmap. Explain milestones and how firm each date is. You produce a client-readable roadmap view.
  7. Agree working rules. Settle records, meeting rhythm, response times, change, escalation, and where restricted information may live. You produce a communication and escalation plan.
  8. Set installation and training dates. Book environment work and a weekly training rhythm that covers every role and shift. Prefer a go-live date in the clinic's least busy period. You produce a calendar.
  9. Review risks, decisions, and dependencies. State each one as condition, effect, owner, next action, and date.
  10. Handle disagreement. Tie each disagreement to a decision, the person with authority, and a date. Do not make up product promises.
  11. Close, record, and issue. Read back decisions and actions, and get the project owner's confirmation. Link every checked section to its evidence. Send the record within one business day and put all dates on the shared calendar.

You will produce:

  • an approved kickoff agenda and participant list;
  • a completed kickoff record with decisions, actions, owners, and dates;
  • a communication and escalation plan;
  • an installation and environment-readiness schedule;
  • a weekly training and decision calendar.

Checked off when: the client project owner has confirmed the read-back, and the kickoff record, working rules, and calendar are issued with named owners and dates.

The problem

Cedar Point Internal Medicine bought a new electronic health record (EHR) system for its two locations, Cedar Point main and Lakeshore. The kickoff is set for Thursday. On Monday you notice three things. The sales representative says Lakeshore is included, but the signed order lists only Cedar Point main. The sponsor has already told staff that go-live is February 2, but the old vendor has not confirmed a date for the data export. The practice manager has invited all 22 staff and wants every open question settled "in the room."

Before you read on, try these:

1. Should this kickoff go ahead on Thursday? What would you need to know to decide?

2. How would you describe the February 2 date to the client?
