Module 3 — Answers

Answers for this chapter

Check yourself

Is your own team ready before the client joins?

  1. Any four of: the controlling agreement and scope summary; purchased outcomes and exclusions; the go-live date and how firm it is; the roadmap and major dependencies; the internal contact for each workstream; questions for the client; decisions the team can make on the call; information to keep out of a general meeting; the first next steps.
  2. Yes. A missing test date is a minor gap. Go ahead, name it as an open item, and give it a decision date.
  3. Reschedule if the gap cannot be fixed in time. The team cannot say what was bought, so fix the handoff with sales and your manager first.

Who should be in the room?

  1. Any four of: executive sponsor, client project owner, implementation specialist, sales representative, active workstream leads, and specialists whose authority a kickoff decision needs.
  2. More people means more talk and less clear authority. Decisions take longer, and the people who must decide get less time.
  3. Invite the billing manager, or move the billing decision to a meeting where the billing manager is present. The decision decides the invitation.

Run the meeting in a fixed order

  1. Opening and purpose; outcome and scope; team and authority; roadmap and milestones; working rules; installation and training schedule; risks, decisions, and dependencies; next actions and close.
  2. A memorized order frees your attention for the people and decisions in the room. It also makes kickoffs consistent across clients.
  3. Move that decision to the start of the meeting and handle it while she is present.

Confirm the outcome, the scope, and the reason for change

  1. A new request is something the client now wants that was not sold; it goes to the change process. A misunderstanding is a belief that something was sold when the documents disagree; the commercial owner resolves it.
  2. The clinic's leaders own the change. Staff listen to their own leaders, and the implementer does not own the clinic's culture.
  3. Record it as a new request, send it to the change process, and continue the agenda.

Name the team and the escalation bypass

  1. Any four of: sponsor, project owner, workflow decision-makers, data owner, training coordinator, required approval roles.
  2. Urgent safety, privacy, or security concerns can get stuck behind one person. Some concerns also need an independent route.
  3. The escalation bypass, straight to the clinic's privacy authority.

Say how firm each date is

  1. What the date is based on, what makes it possible, and what could move it.
  2. The client plans staff, patients, and leases around it. If it moves, the client loses trust and may face real costs.
  3. Say it is contractual and at risk, because the export date is not confirmed.

Agree the working rules

  1. Any five of: where status and documents live; who approves scope, workflow, data, and go-live; how actions and decisions are recorded; status-meeting frequency; response times; change process; escalation route; where restricted information may and may not be stored.
  2. Restricted information, such as patient data, must stay in approved storage. Without a rule, it ends up in project boards and email.
  3. Decide which record is official, and name who keeps the client-facing copy matching it.

Set installation and training dates

  1. Any five of: environment or install date; prerequisites and contacts; blackout periods; weekly training day and time; roles per subject; recording or attendance policy; location or platform; time for homework, correction, and QC.
  2. A steady time improves attendance and makes planning easier.
  3. Keep the main session and add a repeated role-based session at a time the late shift can attend, or use approved recordings.

State risks so someone can act

  1. Condition, possible effect, owner, next action, and decision or due date.
  2. The kickoff is for items that need the client's awareness, action, or authority. Internal-only items waste client time.
  3. Example: "Claim enrollment forms for the three largest payers are not signed. Claim testing on November 3 may slip. The billing manager signs them by October 15 and confirms in the project record."

Handle disagreement without hiding it

  1. Restate neutrally; name who decides; state missing information; record options and effects; decide now if the right people and evidence are present; otherwise assign analysis and a decision date.
  2. The people voting may lack the authority and knowledge. A vote can create an unsafe decision with no accountable owner.
  3. Say you will record the need and answer after you check with the product team, then give a date for the answer.

Close, record, and issue the kickoff record

  1. Any five of: decisions; open questions; actions, owners, and dates; installation date; meeting and training times; next meeting; next milestone.
  2. It proves only that someone clicked a box. It does not show what was agreed or who agreed.
  3. Replace it with a set date, the prerequisites, and a named owner.

Worked example prompts

  1. Moving the decision. A decision made while the approver is present is final and recorded in the read-back. An email approval can be delayed, forgotten, or answered without the discussion.
  2. Naming the four dependencies. Named dependencies tell the sponsor exactly what could move January and who must act. "At risk" alone gives no task.
  3. Sending the record the next morning. Memories of a meeting drift fast. A record sent within one business day lets people correct it while the meeting is fresh, and lets work start.

Faded example

  1. Produced: a revised agenda with the lead pediatrician's decisions at the start. Why: only she can approve them, and she will not be present later.
  2. What the specialist did: recorded online bill pay as a new request and sent it to the change process; the meeting continued. Produced: confirmed scope and one change request. (Also ask the lead pediatrician for the reason-for-change message.)
  3. April 7 is contractual and at risk, because the export date from the old vendor is not confirmed.
  4. A weekly main session plus a repeated role-based session for the second shift, or approved recordings.
  5. "The old vendor has not confirmed the export date. Test conversion and the April 7 go-live may slip. The office manager contacts the vendor this week and reports the date by Friday."
  6. A kickoff record with each section linked to evidence, confirmed by the office manager at read-back, sent within one business day, with all dates on the shared calendar.

Knowledge check

  1. The team should agree on the controlling agreement, scope, outcomes and exclusions, the go-live date and how firm it is, the roadmap, workstream contacts, and the questions and decisions for the call. If the team cannot say what was bought, who owns the project, or what happens next, it should reschedule and fix the handoff.
  2. The kickoff exists to make decisions, so it needs the people with authority over those decisions. A large audience slows the meeting and blurs who decides. Others learn about the project through the resource map and later meetings.
  3. Record it as a new request and send it to the change process, then continue the kickoff. If the client believes it was already sold, log it as a misunderstanding of the sale for the commercial owner instead.
  4. State the basis of each date: contractual, target, or dependent on someone else. Say what makes it possible and what could move it. Do not present a target as a commitment.
  5. When one time cannot reach every role or shift, such as a multi-shift clinic or a desk staffed by one person. Keep the rhythm with repeated role-based sessions, alternating times, or approved recordings.
  6. A link to the record that proves the result: the acknowledged scope, the role list, the dated calendar, or the log entries with owners. A bare checkmark proves only that someone clicked it.

Spaced review

  1. Operational. The date is tied to a business event, so moving it means changing that event or planning around it. It cannot simply be adjusted like a target.
  2. Name one accountable role, or split the row into two questions: for example, billing approves the code and charge path, and IT approves the technical setup. Shared accountability means no one is truly accountable.
  3. It is a remembered commitment. List it as to be confirmed, not in scope, with the sales representative as owner and a due date.
  4. An issue. The mismatch already exists today. It is not a guess about the future (risk) or an unchecked belief (assumption).
  5. Record each role separately and name the person as accountable for each. The roles stay visible even when one person fills them.
