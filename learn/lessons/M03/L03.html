Module 3, Lesson 3: Close, record, and issue the kickoff record

Close, record, and issue the kickoff record

A meeting that ends without a confirmed record leaves each person with a different memory. The read-back and the issued record make one shared version.

At the close, read back:

  • decisions made;
  • questions still open;
  • actions, owners, and due dates;
  • the installation or environment date;
  • meeting and training times;
  • the next meeting;
  • the next milestone.

Ask the client project owner to confirm the read-back. End on time if you can. Do not leave a key ownership question open just to protect the calendar.

Then check off each section in the project system, and attach evidence to each check. A useful kickoff record shows:

Section Status Evidence or result
Outcome and scope Confirmed / open Link to acknowledged scope and open decisions
Team and authority Confirmed / open Named role list
Roadmap Reviewed Version and date
Working rules Confirmed Communication and escalation plan
Installation Scheduled / pending Date, prerequisites, owner
Training rhythm Scheduled / pending Calendar and role groups
Risks and decisions Reviewed Log items and owners
Next actions Assigned Action register

A checked box alone proves only that someone clicked it. The linked record shows what happened.

Send the decision and action record within one business day. Put installation, meetings, training, and due dates on the shared calendar.

Rule: Link every checked section to evidence, and issue the record within one business day.

Compare two cases. At Cedar Point, the "Training rhythm" row shows only a checkmark. Before you issue the record, you link it to the calendar and role groups. At a pediatric clinic, every row is linked, but the kickoff ends late on a Friday. You send the record on Monday, the next business day. The shared rule: the client gets a record that proves each check, before memories drift apart.

Check yourself

  1. List five items you read back at the close.
  2. Why is a bare checkmark weak evidence?
  3. The installation row says "early November." What do you fix before issuing the record?

Worked example: kickoff at a two-location outpatient clinic

A two-location outpatient clinic bought online intake and an EHR for both sites. The sponsor hopes for a January go-live. Here is what the implementation specialist did.

  1. Held the readiness review. The specialist met the sales representative and the internal leads. Produced: a readiness checklist with one open item: the clearinghouse enrollment had not started. Why: the purchase, owner, and next steps were clear, so a named open item did not justify a reschedule.

  2. Chose participants by purpose. The specialist invited the sponsor, the operations manager, a physician leader, the billing manager, an information-security contact, and a data-validation lead. Produced: a participant list of eight and a note asking about shift needs. Why: each person held a decision the kickoff needed.

  3. Opened and checked for early leavers. The physician leader had to leave after 40 minutes. The specialist moved the clinical-owner decision to the start. Produced: a revised agenda. Why: only the physician leader could accept the clinical-owner role.

Why do you think the specialist moved the decision instead of asking the physician leader to approve it by email later?

  1. Confirmed outcome, scope, and reason for change. The sponsor confirmed online intake and two-site readiness as the main outcomes. She stated the message for staff: "We are changing so patients stop filling out the same form twice." A request for text reminders went to the change process. Produced: confirmed scope, one change request, and a staff message. Why: scope must be confirmed before dates, and the leader owns the message.

  2. Confirmed team and authority. The clinic named the operations manager as project owner, the physician leader for clinical workflow, the billing manager for billing decisions, the security contact, and the data lead. Produced: a named role list with an escalation bypass to security. Why: every later decision needs a named owner.

  3. Walked the roadmap. The specialist called January a target, not a commitment. It depended on the data export, clearinghouse enrollment, a device check, and the staffing calendar. Produced: a roadmap view with a basis label on each date. Why: the sponsor needed to know what could move January.

Why do you think the specialist named the four dependencies instead of just saying "January is at risk"?

  1. Agreed working rules. The team chose the client portal as the official record and set a weekly status meeting. Produced: a communication and escalation plan. Why: one official record prevents two versions of the truth.

  2. Set installation and training dates. Training went on Wednesday afternoons, with a shorter repeat on Thursday mornings for the second shift. The operations manager noted that January is the clinic's flu peak. Produced: a calendar and a note to discuss a later go-live. Why: every shift must attend, and the least busy period is safer for go-live.

  3. Reviewed dependencies. The specialist stated three dependencies, each with condition, effect, owner, next action, and date. Produced: three log entries. Why: each gave the client a task, not a worry.

  4. Read back, recorded, and issued. The operations manager confirmed the read-back. The specialist linked each checked section to its record. Produced: the kickoff record, sent the next morning, with all dates on the shared calendar. Why: the one-business-day limit keeps memories from drifting apart.

Why do you think the specialist sent the record the next morning rather than at the next weekly meeting?

Your turn: faded example

If you have done this task on a real project, try this first, then compare with the worked example.

A single-location pediatric clinic bought a new EHR with a patient portal. Its old vendor will provide a data export. The contract names a go-live date of April 7. The clinic has two shifts.

  1. Held the readiness review. The team confirmed the purchase and the project owner (the office manager). The export date from the old vendor is not yet confirmed. Produced: a readiness checklist with one open item. Why: the purchase and owner were clear, so the kickoff could go ahead with the open item named.

  2. Chose participants by purpose. The specialist invited the sponsor (the lead pediatrician), the office manager, the billing lead, and the vendor's data-conversion lead. Produced: a participant list and a question about shift needs. Why: each person held a kickoff decision.

  3. Opened and checked for early leavers. The lead pediatrician has clinic patients after the first hour. Produced: __________. Why: __________.

  4. Confirmed outcome, scope, and reason for change. The office manager asks to add online bill pay. It is not in the order, and no one says it was sold. What the specialist did: __________. Produced: __________.

  5. Walked the roadmap. How should the specialist describe April 7? __________.

  6. Set training dates. What rhythm fits a clinic with two shifts? __________.

  7. Stated the export risk. Write it in the five-part form: __________.

  8. Closed and issued. Produced, and by when: __________.

Field assignment

Work from the practice case, not a real client or real data. Use Cedar Point Internal Medicine (fictional; two locations, Cedar Point main and Lakeshore), or the Month 3 packet in the course simulation casebook. Where the case is silent, write a reasonable assumption and label it as an assumption.

Prepare and role-play, or fully script, the kickoff:

  1. Complete the readiness review.
  2. Choose participants by the decisions and authority required.
  3. Fill in the standard kickoff flow.
  4. Prepare a client-readable roadmap view.
  5. Identify the decisions, dependencies, and risks needing client action.
  6. Run the meeting (role-play or script) and check off each section with evidence.
  7. Issue the decision and action record within one business day.
  8. Place installation, meetings, training sessions, and near-term due dates on the shared calendar.
