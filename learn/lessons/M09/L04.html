Module 9, Lesson 4: Hand monitoring to operations

Hand monitoring to operations

Every integration keeps running after the project team leaves. If no trained person watches the queues, failures pile up unseen.

For each integration, record:

  • who monitors, and who is the backup;
  • dashboard, queue, report, or alert;
  • how often it is reviewed;
  • expected volume and timing;
  • error and aging limits;
  • who may retry an item;
  • vendor escalation;
  • incident route;
  • reconciliation;
  • evidence and retention;
  • planned maintenance and change review.
Do not become the permanent owner by default. Transfer monitoring to an operational role with training, access, and support.

Rule: An integration is done only when a trained staff owner watches its queues.

Check yourself

  1. Name five items in the operational monitoring plan.
  2. Why should the implementer not stay as the monitoring owner?

Worked example: clearinghouse connection

A two-location clinic is preparing its practice management go-live. One synthetic test claim returned a 999 Accepted and a 277CA Accepted. The technical team wants to mark the clearinghouse integration complete.

  1. The specialist checked the enrollment tracker against the approved payer list. Produced: a list of enrollment gaps by payer and location. Why: a clean test claim shows only that claims can go out, not that payments can come back.

  2. The specialist found the gap. Clearinghouse enrollment was active at both locations. At the second location, the second-largest payer showed claims approved, ERA "submitted," and EFT not started. Produced: a note that remittances would arrive on paper and payments by check. Why: the four enrollment steps are separate, so one approval does not cover the others.

    Why do you think the specialist checked enrollment before agreeing that the integration was complete?

  3. The specialist reopened the integration record and logged a dependency. The owner was the clinic's credentialing coordinator. The need date was two weeks before go-live. Produced: a dated, owned dependency entry. Why: the client owns enrollment submissions; the specialist tracks them.

  4. The specialist asked the billing manager for an interim plan. The billing manager decided that a named billing specialist would post paper remittances by hand and reconcile them weekly to deposits. Produced: a recorded interim process. Why: the billing manager owns posting decisions, and go-live should not wait on a gap with a workable plan.

    Why did the specialist ask the billing manager instead of choosing the interim process?

  5. Quality control (QC) tested the 835 path with the clearinghouse's synthetic files. Tests covered a full payment, a partial payment, a denial routed to the denial queue, a zero-pay, and a takeback. They also matched a sample EFT trace number to its 835. Produced: end-to-end payment test evidence. Why: payment failures are as important to test as claim failures.

  6. The team marked the integration complete. Produced: a completed integration record with tests, owners, and the open dependency. Why: every gate item now had evidence.

    What could go wrong if the team had marked the integration complete after the first clean claim?

Your turn: faded example

If you have done this task on a real project, try this first, then compare with the worked example.

A clinic's reference-lab results interface uses HL7 v2 result messages. In testing, every message returns an "accepted" acknowledgement.

  1. The tester compared the business result to the transport result. Two results for a synthetic patient with a common name filed to another patient with the same name and a different birth date. Produced: a failed test step. Why: a transport acknowledgement does not prove the result filed to the right chart.
  2. The tester logged a defect. Produced: a defect record linked to the failed step. Why: a wrong-patient result is a safety failure that needs a tracked fix.
  3. Who decides what happens to results that fail the birth-date match? Produced: ________. Why: ________.
  4. What does the vendor change, and what does QC test afterward? Produced: ________. Why: ________.
  5. What must be in place before the interface is approved? Produced: ________. Why: ________.

Field assignment

Use the Cedar Point Internal Medicine practice case (course casebook, Track A, Month 9 packet). Do not use a real organization, project, or data. Select one integration other than the clearinghouse, such as the payment vendor or the electronic-signature service. Also select two configuration areas.

  1. Complete the integration inventory and owner map.
  2. Draw the data flow and security boundaries.
  3. Record contracts, enrollment, credentials, user accounts, environment, and outside dependencies.
  4. Build a prescriber readiness tracker for EPCS for the case's prescribers, with owners and need dates.
  5. Define acknowledgement, error, retry, duplicate, downtime, and reconciliation behavior.
  6. Write at least ten end-to-end tests with the expected result of each.
  7. Build governed form, code, template, or payment records for your two configuration areas.
  8. Draft a source register entry for one clinical exchange the case needs, such as sending a referral note.
  9. List the technical, business, clinical, privacy, security, and financial approvals needed, and who gives each.
  10. Assign operational monitoring and support.

Knowledge check

  1. Why is a successful connection not the same as a successful business workflow?
  2. What is the difference between transport acknowledgement and business acceptance?
  3. What does a 999 tell you, and what does a 277CA tell you?
  4. How is a rejection different from a denial, and how is each one worked?
  5. Why must clearinghouse enrollment and each payer's ERA and EFT enrollment be tracked separately?
  6. Why does "supports USCDI" need a version number, and where would you check certification?
  7. Who decides whether a configuration choice could be information blocking?
  8. Why must both endpoints have owners?
  9. What controls prevent forms, codes, and templates from becoming inconsistent?
  10. What must be transferred to operations before an integration is complete?
  11. Which EPCS steps must be done for each prescriber, and who completes them?
