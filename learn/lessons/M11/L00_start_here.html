Module 11 — Start here: outline and the problem

Chapter outline

  1. Walk the whole operating day with the people who will run it, including failure and downtime paths. You produce an end-to-end journey review.
  2. Walk the site. With the clinic's information technology (IT) owner, check every device, printer, and network area. You produce a site readiness record with owners for failures.
  3. Simplify without removing controls. You produce a list of approved simplifications and the retests they needed.
  4. Make every queue, alert, and reminder accountable. You produce a register with trigger, owner, aging rule, escalation, and backup.
  5. Put quality measures inside the workflow and capture the baseline. You produce measure definitions, owners, thresholds, actions, and pre-live baseline values.
  6. Build audit-ready evidence and test retrieval. You produce an audit index that someone else can follow.
  7. Set recovery objectives and get them approved. Get written approval from named owners and confirm a restore test. You produce an approved recovery record.
  8. Check security readiness against a recognized framework. Use the federal 405(d) practices and the Cybersecurity Framework 2.0 of the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST). You produce a security readiness checklist with owners.
  9. Define go-live support. You produce an onsite and remote support roster, including client super users by location and shift.
  10. Agree the go-live schedule plan. The clinic decides how far to reduce appointments and for how long. You produce a recorded schedule decision.
  11. Build the command and communication plan. You produce issue, triage, escalation, and decision procedures, and a patient and partner notice list.
  12. Rehearse cutover, a serious failure, and a full patient visit. You produce timed rehearsal records with corrections.
  13. Run final quality assurance (QA) on the non-production system. Include user sign-in and prescribing readiness. You produce a final QA report.
  14. Confirm training is current. You produce a training roster that shows each user is competent on the release going live.
  15. Build the readiness dashboard. Report by area, not by one percent. You produce a dashboard that names each blocker and owner.
  16. Offer a pre-live SAFER review (Safety Assurance Factors for Electronic Health Record Resilience). The clinic rates; you bring evidence.
  17. Prepare the go/no-go decision. You assemble the facts and a recommendation. The authorized leaders decide, and you record it.

You will produce:

  • an operational readiness review with a site record and an accountable queue, measure, baseline, and evidence register;
  • an approved recovery record and a security readiness checklist;
  • a support roster, schedule decision, command and communication plan, and rehearsal records;
  • a final QA report, current training roster, readiness dashboard, and recorded go/no-go decision.

Checked off when: authorized leaders have recorded a go-live decision, and no must-pass blocker is hidden behind a percent-complete figure.

The problem

Cedar Point Internal Medicine goes live in three weeks. The project plan shows 96 percent of tasks done. The sponsor wants to announce the date to staff tomorrow.

You walk the Lakeshore site on Tuesday. The prescription printer in exam room 3 does not print. Two providers took training four months ago, before the results screens changed. Nobody has told the reference lab or the patients. The full schedule for go-live week is already booked.

Pre-questions: Is Cedar Point 96 percent ready? Who decides whether to cut appointments during go-live week?
