Module 4, Lesson 5: Field assignment

Field assignment

Work from the practice case, not a real client or real data. Use Cedar Point Internal Medicine (fictional; two locations, Cedar Point main and Lakeshore), or the Month 4 packet in the course simulation casebook. Where the case is silent, write a reasonable assumption and label it as an assumption.

Choose one high-value workflow:

  1. Write the discovery plan and participant list.
  2. Role-play or script current-state discovery.
  3. Map the normal path, decisions, handoffs, waits, controls, and at least three meaningful exceptions.
  4. Separate requirements from habits and record items needing specialist confirmation.
  5. Record the accessibility entry and any sensitive-record questions for the workflow, each with its decision owner.
  6. Design the future state with roles, access, evidence, follow-up, and downtime where relevant.
  7. Write at least five traceable requirements with acceptance evidence.
  8. Create six acceptance scenarios.
  9. Create the high-level solution blueprint, environment plan (with compensating controls if any environment is missing), and first build inventory for the workflow.
  10. Conduct a walkthrough and record approval or the corrections required.
  11. Update the roadmap, risks, decisions, and change record.

Self-check rubric. Your work is ready when you can answer yes to each:

Knowledge check

  1. Why should discovery begin with a real current workflow rather than a feature wish list?
  2. What information makes a workflow map usable outside the meeting?
  3. How does the implementer distinguish policy from habit?
  4. What is requirements traceability, and why does it matter?
  5. What accessibility facts should you record, and why is a vendor ACR not enough on its own?
  6. Which sensitive-record questions must be asked in discovery, and who decides each one?
  7. When should the first implementation preserve a familiar process, and when should it not?
  8. Why are exception and downtime scenarios required before approval?
  9. What additional decisions are needed to turn a future-state map into a controlled build?
  10. How can a project compensate when the vendor has no separate test or training environment?

Spaced review

These questions come from earlier modules. Answer them without looking back.

  1. (Module 3) In a discovery session, a manager says the lab interface "will be live by March 9 for sure." The lab has not issued credentials. How should that date be described to the client?
  2. (Module 3) Two physicians disagree in a design session about who signs off a referral. The medical director is absent. What do you do?
  3. (Module 2) A nurse in discovery asks for a new feature that was never in the plan. Why can it not go straight into the build, and where does it go?
  4. (Module 1) During discovery, the practice manager says sales promised a custom referral report. The signed order does not list it. How do you treat this promise?
  5. (Module 1) Discovery shows the clinic uses signature pads at check-in. Which resource does that item create, and what is the first question to ask?

Module 4 gate — future-state workflow approved

Who must be in the room for approval: the accountable client owner, plus the specialists whose decisions are still open.

Keep in your course workbook: discovery plan, current- and future-state maps, requirements traceability record, accessibility entry, sensitive-record decisions, acceptance scenarios, decision log, workflow approval, high-level solution blueprint, environment plan, and build handoff. This is practice evidence for the module. The EHRIC capstone is a separate set of three artifacts on its own case; Module 12 and the capstone candidate packet describe it.
