Module 4 — Start here: outline and the problem

Chapter outline

This chapter teaches you to learn how the client's work really happens, design the approved new workflow, and hand it into a controlled build. The steps are in the order the chapter teaches them.

  1. Observe real work. Ask the people who do the work to show a real example. Do not start with a wish list.
  2. Plan discovery by end-to-end workflow. Organize sessions from trigger to outcome, with scope, participants, scenarios, and decision owners. You produce a discovery plan.
  3. Map the current state. Draw roles, steps, decisions, handoffs, waits, and controls with a legend. You produce validated current-state maps.
  4. Ask for the exceptions. Record the approved path for missing data, failed interfaces, downtime, and absent approvers.
  5. Separate policy from habit. Record where each "must" comes from and who confirms it.
  6. Write requirements with evidence. State who, what, when, which control, and how it will be verified. Trace each one from purchased outcome to approval.
  7. Ask about accessibility. Record the legal or contract target, the WCAG version and level, and the vendor's accessibility report. Name who decides.
  8. Design the future state. Work through a ten-step order, from outcome to approval. You produce future-state maps.
  9. Control access by role. Record what each role may view, create, change, approve, export, or delete.
  10. Plan for sensitive records. Ask about psychotherapy notes, substance use disorder records, minors, guardians, and proxy portal access. Route each rule to its owner.
  11. Keep human authority. The system routes and records; people decide.
  12. Balance continuity and improvement. Sort each proposed change into one of five classes.
  13. Hand the design into a controlled build. Produce a solution blueprint and build inventory.
  14. Plan environments. If the vendor has no separate test or training environment, record the risk and the controls that make up for it. You produce an environment plan.
  15. Validate with scenarios. Walk normal, failure, access, handoff, and downtime cases. You produce acceptance scenarios.
  16. Run the approval meeting. Get a versioned approval from the accountable owner.

You will produce:

  • a discovery plan and current- and future-state maps;
  • a requirements and decision record, with accessibility and sensitive-record decisions;
  • acceptance scenarios;
  • a solution blueprint, environment plan, and build handoff.

Checked off when: the accountable client owner has approved a versioned future-state design, and every specialist question is either decided or open with an owner and date.

The problem

At Cedar Point Internal Medicine, the practice manager hands you a list of 40 features she wants in the new system. "This is how we want check-in to work," she says. Later, a front-desk worker at Lakeshore shows you her real day. Patients fill out paper forms. She types the forms into the old system. Sometimes she attaches a scanned history to the wrong patient when two names are alike. She also says, "We have to photocopy every insurance card. It's the law." No one knows where that rule comes from.

Before you read on, try these:

1. Which should shape the new check-in design: the manager's feature list or the front-desk worker's real day? Why?

2. What would you do with the photocopy rule?
