Module 8, Lesson 5: Field assignment

Field assignment

Build a conversion plan for the Cedar Point Internal Medicine practice case (fictional; two locations, Cedar Point main and Lakeshore). Use a simulated source with synthetic data only. Do not use a real client, employer, or patient.

  1. Inventory sources, owners, formats, populations, risks, access, and retention needs.
  2. Classify structured data, documents, manual information, derived values, relationships, audit history, and exclusions.
  3. Define the approved treatment for each category.
  4. Profile a sample or simulated source.
  5. Build at least fifteen mapping rows with transformation and validation rules.
  6. Write the secure-transfer and staging plan.
  7. Plan at least two test conversions before production.
  8. Define count, total, field, relationship, scenario, access, and client validation.
  9. Draft a clinical validation rule for medications, allergies, problems, and immunizations for the clinical authority to approve, with the flag report, pre-visit worklist, and first-visit reconciliation tracking.
  10. Write production cutover, delta, fallback, communication, archive, and retirement steps.
  11. Role-play the required data, clinical, privacy and security, and release approvals.

Self-check rubric. Your work is ready when:

Capstone link: capstone Artifact C (the go/no-go decision record) draws on readiness evidence that includes conversion results. Its instructions are in the capstone candidate packet. Build it from the packet's facts, not from this chapter's examples.

Knowledge check

  1. Why are discrete, manual, and scanned categories useful but insufficient by themselves?
  2. What is the risk of mapping fields by name alone?
  3. What should happen when there is no safe destination field?
  4. Why must converted relationships be validated as well as values?
  5. What is the difference between technical reconciliation and client validation?
  6. What must be defined before the team can rely on a fallback or rollback plan?
  7. Why can a converted allergy list pass count reconciliation and still be unsafe?
  8. Who approves the clinical validation rule, and what does the implementer do and not do?

Spaced review

These questions come from earlier modules. Answer them without looking back.

  1. (Module 7) The vendor fixes a mapping row in version 3.3 so stopped medications no longer convert as active. The clinical sample now looks right. What else must you run before the defect closes, and why?
  2. (Module 7) Mapping version 3.2 passed all checks in the test environment. The production load will use version 3.3. Can you reuse the 3.2 results? Explain.
  3. (Module 5) In a training class on converted data, a learner opens a patient's scanned history and finds another patient's documents. How do you sort this item, and where does it go?
  4. (Module 4) The old system holds psychotherapy notes for Lakeshore's social worker. Who decides who may see them in the new system, and what is your job?
  5. (Module 2) The old system's vendor will provide the final export but has not agreed to a date. How do you record this on the roadmap?
