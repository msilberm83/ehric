Module 8 — Start here: outline and the problem

Chapter outline

This chapter moves approved information from the old system to the new one. The steps below follow the order taught.

  1. Name owners and authority. Identify who approves scope, meaning, access, retention, and retirement. You produce an ownership list.
  2. Inventory the sources. List every system, file, and paper source with its owner, volume, format, and restrictions. You produce a source inventory.
  3. Classify the information. Sort it into structured data, documents, manual items, derived values, relationships, audit history, and exclusions. You produce a classification.
  4. Set expectations and define scope. Tell the client what will and will not move, and get each population and treatment approved. You produce a scope decision.
  5. Profile and clean. Measure the source and record owned rules for every correction. You produce a profile and cleaning log.
  6. Build the mapping workbook. Map each field by meaning, not name, with versioned transformation rules. You produce an approved mapping workbook.
  7. Protect the data. Plan secure transfer, staging, access, error files, and disposal of temporary copies. You produce a security plan.
  8. Run repeated test conversions. Load samples, then full volume, under controlled mapping versions. You produce test-run reports.
  9. Reconcile and validate with the owners. Explain every count difference, then have named owners validate values, relationships, access, and workflows. You produce reconciliation and client-validation evidence.
  10. Clinically validate clinical lists. Flag uncoded medications, allergies, problems, and immunizations, and set up pre-visit review and first-visit reconciliation under the clinical authority's rule. You produce an approved clinical validation rule and tracking.
  11. Plan the cutover. Define freeze, delta, load order, go/no-go criteria, and a real fallback. You produce a cutover plan.
  12. Archive and retire the source. Confirm required history stays accessible before anyone retires the old system. You produce a retirement record.

You will produce:

  • a source inventory, scope decision, and approved mapping workbook;
  • a security plan and test-conversion reports with explained reconciliation;
  • client and clinical validation evidence;
  • a production cutover, delta, fallback, archive, and retirement plan.

Checked off when: the data and clinical owners have recorded acceptance or open defects for a tested conversion, and the cutover and retirement rules are documented and approved.

The problem

Cedar Point Internal Medicine is moving Lakeshore's patients from an old system into the new one. After the final rehearsal, the vendor's report says 100% of records loaded with no errors. The practice manager wants to mark the conversion ready.

You open a few converted charts. One patient had "PCN - hives" (PCN is shorthand for penicillin) typed as free text in the old allergy list. In the new system, the allergy shows as text, but the prescribing alert does not fire when you order penicillin for a test patient.

Before you read on: Can a conversion that loads every record still be unsafe? Who decides whether a converted allergy list is safe for patient care?
