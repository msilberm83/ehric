Module 6 — Start here: outline and the problem

Chapter outline

  1. Name the domain authority. For every specialized workflow, name the clinical, privacy, records, billing, or safety leader who owns it. You produce an authority list with an evaluator for each workflow.
  2. Recheck readiness. Confirm the workflow, scenarios, configuration, access, and QC are approved before training. If a high-impact decision is still open, hold a decision session instead of a class. Hold final training close to go-live, on the tested release (Module 5).
  3. Train triage as a routing process. Teach who receives, documents, routes, and acknowledges. Clinical leaders own the protocol and the clinical judgment.
  4. Train visit documentation. Cover templates, carried-forward information, signing, amendments, and wrong-record correction. A finished note is not automatically a billable claim.
  5. Close the loop on orders and results. Follow each order from entry to closure. Name who watches failures, unmatched results, and overdue follow-up. Include converted medication and allergy lists in provider scenarios.
  6. Ask disciplined questions about specialty work. Build scenarios with the specialist who owns the work. For behavioral health, ask who decides on separate psychotherapy notes, federal substance use rules, and who may see each note. You produce approved scenario sets and access tests.
  7. Follow the information downstream. For each scenario, trace what the record starts later, who notices a missed handoff, and what QC will check.
  8. Use progressive practice. Move from orientation to demonstration, guided, independent, and exception practice, then teach-back. The approved evaluator makes the competence decision.
  9. Route what training reveals. Send each defect, decision, change, and homework item through the controlled path from Module 5. A spelling fix in a plain label can follow the low-risk process. A clinical template or access change goes to its owner.
  10. Collect approvals. Get domain approval and QC evidence before the module closes.

You will produce:

  • specialized training plans and scenario sets approved by the domain owners;
  • competence and remediation records, signed by the approved evaluator;
  • a controlled register of defects, decisions, changes, and homework;
  • domain approvals and QC evidence.

Checked off when: each required specialized role has practiced normal, exception, and failure scenarios in an approved build, the approved evaluator has recorded competence, and every clinical, privacy, and billing decision stays with its owner.

The problem

Cedar Point Internal Medicine is adding a licensed clinical social worker at Lakeshore. Nurse triage training is booked for Tuesday. The medical director has not chosen the after-hours escalation rule yet. The social worker wants her therapy notes hidden from everyone else. A physician asks you to sign off the providers as "competent" so they can stop coming to class. The practice manager adds, "You've done this at other clinics. Just tell us what to do."

Before you read: Which of these decisions are yours to make? Who should decide whether a provider is competent?
